# Sequence Structures

Use two or three emails. Each step must add a distinct reason to care.

## Two-email sequence

### Email 1: relevance and outcome

Use a verified role, event, or workflow. Name one problem and one outcome. Add one proof point only
when sourced. End with one low-effort CTA.

### Email 2: useful detail

Add a checklist, example, risk, or implementation detail. Do not repeat the opener or full value
proposition. End with one CTA that fits the new angle.

## Three-email sequence

### Email 1: relevance and problem

Connect one verified fact or responsible workflow to one likely problem.

### Email 2: proof or method

Add approved proof or explain a concrete method. If proof is not available, use a practical artifact.

### Email 3: close or route

Summarize the relevance in one line and ask whether to close the thread or contact another owner.
Do not create false urgency.

## Sequence controls

- Keep one campaign angle across all steps.
- Change the support, not the core promise.
- Use 75 to 90 words per email by default.
- Use one CTA per step.
- Stop when the user specifies a stop condition or the prospect opts out.
