# Campaign Patterns

Choose one pattern. Do not combine several weak angles in one email.

## Role transition

Use when a verified role change makes a responsibility timely. State the change plainly, connect it
to a likely first priority, and offer one useful next step. Do not assume the person's internal plan.

## Hiring pressure

Use when a current job opening supports the angle. Connect the role to workload, process, or ramp
time. Do not claim that hiring proves budget, urgency, or a specific problem.

## Workflow friction

Use when the persona owns a known workflow. Describe one repeated task and one practical outcome.
Phrase unsourced pain as a question or possibility.

## Peer proof

Use a customer or peer example only when it is approved and sourced. Match the problem and operating
context, not only the industry label. Never imply that the prospect uses the same process.

## Useful artifact

Offer a checklist, comparison, teardown, or short example that helps before a meeting. Name what the
artifact contains. The CTA asks whether to send it.

## Change or event

Use one verified, recent event. State the date-sensitive fact without exaggeration. Connect it to a
reasonable operational consequence and make uncertainty visible.

## Routing question

Use when ownership is uncertain. Briefly state the topic, then ask who owns it. Do not add a second
meeting request.
